Lin Shen

沈琳 · Theoretical & Applied Linguistics

Lin is a PhD student in Theoretical and Applied Linguistics at the University of Cambridge. Before beginning her doctoral studies in October 2024, she earned a master's degree in Translation Studies from Beijing Foreign Studies University (2021–2024) and a bachelor's degree in Translation from Guangdong University of Foreign Studies (2017–2021).

She is fascinated by translation and second language acquisition, and enjoys analysing both translated texts and learner language from sociocultural and cognitive perspectives. More broadly, she is interested in how translation and L2 acquisition shape and reflect society, culture, and cognition.

01 / Research

What I think about

My doctoral thesis — Constrained communication of motion events: Mediation, typological proximity, and proficiency — sits at the intersection of bilingual cognition, translation studies, and language acquisition.

Second Language Acquisition

How learners come to express motion, manner, and path in a typologically distant L2 — and what their detours reveal.

Cognitive Linguistics

Thinking-for-speaking, conceptual transfer, and the cognitive load of moving meaning across typologies.

Corpus Linguistics

Large diachronic and parallel corpora — UN General Debate, political interpreting, learner production — analysed with R and Python.

Translation Studies

Constrained communication, mediation effects, and the linguistic fingerprints that distinguish translated from native discourse.
